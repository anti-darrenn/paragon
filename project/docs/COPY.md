# Tagline options (PLAN.md P8.1)

The current line, "Learning the right way.", says nothing a student can check. The
review's suggested positioning is *the honest WAEC coach*: a likely grade, why students
lose marks, and the shortest path up. Paragon can now back every word of that:
- the estimated grade range;
- the examiner insights;
- the shortest path ranked by real past-paper frequency;
- past questions and practice labelled apart.

Each option has a note on what it promises, so nothing ships that the app can't keep.
**The owner picks one**, and it goes into the splash (`web/index.html`), the
`<meta>`/OG description, the static `/past-questions/` index, and the welcome screen.

1. **"WAEC practice that tells you your grade, and how to raise it."**
   The review's own line. It promises an estimate; the app always labels it an
   estimate, so the copy should never drop the word in longer text.

2. **"Know your grade. Know your next topic."**
   Short enough for the splash and a WhatsApp preview. "Know" is strong for an
   estimate; it pairs best with a subline that says *estimated from your practice*.

3. **"The honest WAEC coach."**
   With the subline "Real past questions, an estimated grade, and the shortest path to
   a better one." This leads on trust, which is the point of the last two weeks of work.

4. **"Practise what WAEC asks most."**
   A concrete, checkable promise: the shortest path is ranked by real past-paper
   frequency. It says nothing about grades, so it stays safe while the content is thin.

5. **"Free WAEC prep that shows you where the marks are."**
   Leads with free, which matters to the audience. "Where the marks are" covers topic
   weights and examiner insights without promising a grade.

**Recommendation: 3, with its subline.** It is the only one that names the
differentiator ("honest") and stays true even where a subject has no past papers yet.
